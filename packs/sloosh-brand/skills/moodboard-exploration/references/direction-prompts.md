# Direction prompts

Use this file to write three or four contrasting directions and one short tile prompt for each.

## Axes to push apart

Two directions must differ on at least two of these axes. Palette and light matter most.

| Axis | Examples |
|---|---|
| Palette | clinical white and pale grey; sand, sage and terracotta; near-black with one jewel tone; saturated primaries |
| Light | soft window light; hard noon sun with crisp shadows; low-key studio with one rim light; flat even light |
| Set | seamless paper; natural material such as linen, stone or wood; a real room; an abstract colour block |
| Props | none; dried botanicals; raw ingredients; geometric shapes; everyday objects in use |
| Lens feel | clean digital; medium-format film; wide and close; long lens with soft background |
| Mood | calm and precise; warm and crafted; moody and editorial; bright and playful |

## Starter set

Use these four when the user gives no mood. Change them to fit the product.

- Clinical white: pale grey and white, flat even light, seamless paper, no props, clean digital.
- Warm botanical: sand, sage and terracotta, soft late-afternoon window light, linen, dried stems, film look.
- Dark editorial: near-black with one jewel tone, one hard rim light, stone, no props, long lens.
- Playful colour: saturated primaries, hard sun with crisp shadows, colour blocks, geometric shapes, wide and close.

## Tile prompt grammar

Keep each prompt to two lines. Detail is for the final image, not the tile.

```
Moodboard tile, direction "[warm botanical]": [the product or a simple silhouette of it] on [sand linen], [dried eucalyptus], [soft late-afternoon window light], palette [sand, sage, terracotta], [medium-format film look], square, no text, no logos, no people.
```

- With a product photo as a reference, write "the product from reference 1" instead of describing it.
- Do not repeat the same set, prop or light words across directions. Shared words make the tiles look the same.
- Do not name an artist, a photographer or another brand.

## Angle study

Use this when the user wants one scene seen from several camera angles. Keep the palette, light, set and props the same in every tile. Change only the camera angle and the lens feel, such as straight on, top down, low three-quarter and close detail. Name the angle in each tile prompt.

## Direction note

After the user picks, write the note in this shape. Keep it under 60 words so it fits in the next prompt.

```
Direction: [name]. Palette: [three to five colours]. Light: [one phrase]. Set: [one phrase]. Props: [one phrase or none]. Lens feel: [one phrase]. Mood: [three adjectives]. Avoid: [what the user disliked in other tiles].
```

## Quality bar

- The directions look clearly different in palette and light, not only in props.
- Each tile has no added text, no logos and no recognisable people.
- Each tile follows the brand kit's never list.
- The direction note is short enough to paste into the next prompt.
- Name tiles by product, direction and version, such as candle mood botanical v1.
