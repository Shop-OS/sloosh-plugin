# Copy rules

Read this file before you write the copy sheet. It holds the platform limits, the hook moves, the awareness treatments, the evidence rules and the banned patterns.

## Platform limits

### Meta, TikTok and Google Performance Max

Keep the copy inside these limits:

- Meta primary text: hard maximum of 2,200 characters. About the first 125 show before "See more".
- Meta link description: recommended up to 27 characters.
- Facebook feed: headline recommended up to 27 characters. Primary text of 50 to 150 characters is recommended.
- Instagram feed: headline recommended up to 40 characters. Primary text recommended up to 125 characters.
- Instagram Reels: primary text of about 44 characters is recommended. Keep the headline short.
- Facebook Reels: no confirmed figure. Keep it short, as for Instagram Reels.
- Stories: primary text recommended up to 125 characters. Facebook Stories headline recommended up to 40 characters. Keep the Instagram Stories headline short.
- TikTok: ad text up to 100 characters, with no links and no curly brackets. Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters and emoji count as 2. TikTok has no headline. Put the display name, up to 20 characters with no emoji, in the headline column.
- Performance Max: 3 to 15 headlines of up to 30 characters. Make at least one headline 15 characters or fewer. Write 1 to 5 long headlines of up to 90 characters and 2 to 5 descriptions of up to 90 characters. Give a business name of up to 25 characters.
- Meta truncates longer text behind "See more". Put the main point first.

### Google Search, LinkedIn and Pinterest

These figures are recommended only. Check the platform's current spec before you rely on them, because they change.

- Google Search responsive ads: headlines of up to 30 characters, up to 15 of them, across at least 8 different themes. Descriptions of up to 90 characters, up to 4 of them.
- LinkedIn: intro text of up to 150 characters shows before the fold. Headline of up to 70 characters.
- Pinterest: title of up to 40 characters. Description of up to 500 characters. Only the first 50 characters show in most views.

### Video hooks

- A TikTok or Reels spoken hook has 8 words or fewer.
- For Reels, Stories and TikTok, keep captions and the product out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% at each side of the frame.

### Text on the image

When another skill puts a line on the image:

- Headline: 6 words or fewer.
- Subline: 10 words or fewer.
- Call to action: 3 words or fewer.
- Story cover: 4 words or fewer.
- Words of 10 characters or fewer render most cleanly.

## Hook moves

| Move | Shape | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Withhold the noun. | Pay it off inside the ad. |
| Specific claim | A concrete claim a result can prove wrong. | It needs proof on screen. |
| Confession | "I was doing X wrong." | It needs a lived detail. |
| Contrast | Two states in the first beat. | Both frames must be true. |
| POV | A very specific situation. | A generic POV is invisible. |
| Exact question | What the buyer types into search. | Use their exact words. |
| Countdown or challenge | Promise a payoff. | The payoff must exist. |

Three-part rule for video: the visual action, the spoken line and the caption each carry a different part of the idea. For a static ad, the headline must not caption the image.

Work in this order: segment, then motivation in the customer's words, then format, then hook. Fill different cells of the matrix. Ten hooks for ten cells beat thirty rewrites of one hook.

## Awareness treatments

| Stage | Treatments |
|---|---|
| Unaware | A feeling. A conversation. |
| Problem-aware | Pain and outcome. A friction tied to a time of day or a moment. |
| Solution-aware | A change of state. A documented statistic. |
| Product-aware | A documented statistic. An objection and its proof. |

The stage is a hypothesis. If it is unknown, write for two stages and say so.

## Evidence

- Language evidence is a complaint or a phrase. It supports a pain angle.
- Claim evidence is a measured result with its population and period. It supports a number.
- Never turn language evidence into a claim.
- Keep a number's precision and its qualifier. A rounded number is fine when it is true.
- Do not invent reviews, ratings, quotes or offers. Do not invent testimonials, names or statistics.

## Banned patterns

These patterns make copy sound machine-written. Remove them.

- "Not X, it's Y", "isn't X, it's Y" and "this isn't about X".
- "No X, no Y, no Z" lists of three.
- Sentences that trail into extra comma clauses.
- Rhetorical questions stacked one after another.
- The words "imagine", "unlock", "elevate", "effortless", "seamless", "game-changing", "revolutionary" and "say goodbye to".
- Em dashes. Use a period or a colon.

Fix each one by stating the thing: what it is, who it is for and what changes.

## Hypothesis

Use this form: If [variant] beats [control] on [metric] by [threshold] for [segment], then [what we learn].

Metrics:

- Hook rate: 3-second views divided by impressions.
- Hold rate: ThruPlays divided by 3-second views.
- Click-through rate.
- Conversion rate.

Change one variable in each comparison.
