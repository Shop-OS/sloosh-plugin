# Sound plan, script and checks

Picture comes first. The sound makes the picture feel finished. Write the plan before any quote, then put it in the Space's brief, mood or instructions fields.

## Sound plan

Write the plan in four lines.

1. Energy arc: how the sound moves from start to end, such as a quiet start, a build and a clean end.
2. Landing: the second where the music lands. Put it on the key visual, such as the product reveal or the logo.
3. Hits: one to three accents. Each hit has a visible cause, such as a cut, a pour or a cap that clicks shut.
4. Silence: a short drop in the music just before the payoff, so the payoff reads louder.

## Music brief

Use this pattern for a mood, genre or brief field:

```
[genre], [mood], about [tempo] beats a minute, [two or three instruments], builds from a quiet start to a lift at [second] seconds, [length] seconds, no vocals, clean ending.
```

- Describe the sound. Do not name a song, an artist or a composer.
- Match the tempo to the cuts. Fast cuts suit 110 to 130 beats a minute. A slow product film suits 70 to 90.
- Ask for no vocals under a voiceover. Sung words fight spoken words.
- Ask for a clean ending, not a fade, when the clip ends on a logo.

## Voiceover script

- Write for the ear. Use short, plain sentences with one idea each.
- Keep each sentence at 12 words or fewer.
- Plan about 2.5 words a second for most lines and fewer for the last line.
- Write numbers the way they are said, such as "twenty percent".
- Spell a brand name the way it is said if it is hard to read.
- Name the product once. Put the call to action last.
- Use punctuation for pauses. A full stop gives a longer pause than a comma.
- Let the voice say something the screen does not show. Do not read the on-screen text aloud.
- Leave about half a second of silence at the start and at the end.

## Mix

- The voice is louder than the music. The music drops under each spoken line.
- No music hit falls on the first spoken word.
- If the clip has its own sound, such as a pour or a click, keep it under the music when the Space allows it.

## Checklist for the finished clip

Give the user this checklist, because you cannot hear the result.

- The music lands on the key visual.
- Each hit has a visible cause.
- Every spoken word is clear, and brand and product names sound right.
- The music does not cover the voice.
- Nothing clips or distorts at the loudest moment.
- The sound ends with the picture, with no cut-off note.
- The clip's own sound is kept or replaced as the user expected.
- The music and the voice are licensed or made by the Space, and every real voice has consent.
