# Carousel frameworks

Five slide plans for product carousels. Adapted and shortened from the carousel frameworks in Corey Haines's marketing-skills (MIT licence). Pick the framework before you write a slide.

| Content | Framework | Why it earns the swipe |
|---|---|---|
| A list of uses, tips or ingredients | A. Value-Stack | An exact count on the cover is a promise the reader can check |
| A result with a system behind it | B. Problem-Proof | Slide 1 makes the claim. The last slide proves it |
| Several named techniques | C. Hack List | Each slide earns the next swipe on its own |
| A strong opinion about a habit | D. Rant Callout | Conviction first, then fairness |
| A product you can show step by step | E. Demo Walkthrough | Showing beats describing |

Two rules apply to every framework:

- Slide 1 competes alone in the feed. Design it as a standalone scroll-stopper.
- Use one visual template for every slide after the cover.

## A. Value-Stack (4 to 10 slides)

- Slide 1, cover: "[N] [things] for [outcome]". N must be the real count.
- Slides 2 to N-1: one item per slide, with 2 to 4 sub-points. Show the product.
- Slide N, last: one action, such as "Comment ROUTINE for the steps" or "Link in bio".

Failure: padding to a bigger number. A tight 6 beats a padded 10.

## B. Problem-Proof (6 to 10 slides)

- Slide 1, hook: a specific result stated as fact. Use only a result the user gives. Add a number only when the user gives one.
- Slide 2: the real problem, named so the reader recognises themselves.
- Slides 3 and 4: the mechanism, with named steps, ingredients or tools.
- Slides 5 to N-1: the detail, the literal routine.
- Slide N, last: the proof, such as a review card the user supplied, a before-and-after photo or a chart.

Failure: vague mechanism slides. Use only proof the user gives.

## C. Hack List (5 to 10 slides)

- Slide 1, cover: the theme and the count.
- Slides 2 to N-1: one hack each. Name the hack in three words. Show it. Give one line of why.
- Slide N, last: restate the best hack and give the call to action.

## D. Rant Callout (5 to 8 slides)

- Slide 1, cover: the opinion, stated plainly.
- Slide 2: what people do instead, and why it fails.
- Slides 3 to N-1: what to do, one move per slide.
- Slide N, last: the product as the easier way, with no hard sell.

Keep it fair. Add one slide that says when the old way is fine. Criticise the habit or the usual way, not a company. Do not name or show a competitor brand or product. Do not make a claim about a competitor's product.

## E. Demo Walkthrough (5 to 8 slides)

- Slide 1, cover: the outcome in one line, with the product in frame.
- Slides 2 to N-1: one step per slide. Show the real product state at each step, such as open, pour, apply and done.
- Slide N, last: the result and the call to action.

## Copy on each slide

- Write a headline of up to 12 words and a supporting line of up to 20 words.
- Use the same type scale on every slide after the cover.
- Show the product on at least half the slides. Never crop the label.
- Give the last slide one action only.

## Keep the template consistent

- Make slide 1 first. Pass it as a reference image for every other slide, with the product photo.
- Use one model for all slides, chosen from the live model list.
- If the look drifts, also pass the previous slide as a reference and shorten the slide's text.
