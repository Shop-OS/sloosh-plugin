# Style guide template

Use this file to write the breakdown, the style guide, the shot list and the do-not-copy list. Use the terms in the glossary, so every skill that reads the plan reads the same words.

## 1. The five-line breakdown

Write these five lines first. Keep each one short.

- Pace: how long each shot holds, and where the pace changes.
- Change: how one scene turns into the next.
- Type: size, weight, case, and how words arrive and leave.
- Colour and light: the palette, the contrast and the grade.
- The one idea: the single visual idea that carries the whole piece.

## 2. The style guide

Fill every field. Write "not shown" when the reference does not show it. Write "estimate" after each value that you did not read from a frame.

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Format | Aspect ratio, total length, and the safe zones the text keeps to. |
| Shot count | The number of shots, the average shot length, and the shortest and longest shot. |
| Rhythm | Where the cuts land: on the beat, off the beat or free. Where the pace speeds up or slows down. |
| Transitions | Each transition type and how many times it appears, with the glossary terms. |
| Camera | Each camera move and how many times it appears, with the glossary terms. |
| Framing | Close, medium or wide. Where the subject sits in the frame. How much empty space the frame leaves for type. |
| Type | Size, weight, case and width. Serif or sans. How words arrive and leave. Where the words sit. How long each line stays on screen. |
| Colour and light | Background, text and accent colours. Use hex codes from the user's brand kit. For colours read from the reference, write the hue in words and mark any hex code as an estimate. |
| Texture and grade | Grain, blur, glow, light leaks, contrast and saturation. |
| Sound | The structure: intro, build, drop and end. The tempo if known. The time of the drop and of each hit that a cut lands on. Voice-over or none. The track and voice of the new piece: owned by the user, licensed or new, never the reference's. |
| The one idea | The same line as in the breakdown. |
| What changes | How the user's subject, words and brand replace the reference's content. |

## 3. The shot list row

Write one row per reference shot, in this shape. The durations add up to the planned length.

```text
Shot 03 · 1.8-2.6 s (0.8 s)
Reference: macro push-in on a hand that lifts a bottle; hard cut on the snare
Camera: slow push-in, shallow focus · In: hard cut · Out: whip pan right
Type: none · Sound: snare hit at 1.8 s
Ours: the serum dropper lifts out of the bottle, label to camera
Route: Sloosh clip
```

Use one of these routes for each row:

- code: type, UI, logos, charts and counters. They must be exact, so code builds them.
- Sloosh still: a hero set, a background or a detail that the edit moves with a slow zoom.
- Sloosh clip: physics, liquid, cloth, light, a product that turns or a person who moves.
- user footage: the user's own photos, screen recordings or clips.
- motion copy: the user's subject performs the reference's moves.

## 4. The do-not-copy list

Name each item that stays out of the new piece:

- The reference's logos, brand marks and watermarks.
- Its copy, captions and on-screen lines.
- Its music, sound effects and voice.
- Its people, faces and characters.
- Its products and its screens.
- Each shot that only looks like theirs. Say how the new shot differs.

Do not imply a partnership, an endorsement or a co-brand that does not exist.

## 5. Glossary

Transitions:

- hard cut: one frame to the next with no effect.
- match cut: a cut that keeps a shape, a motion or a position from one shot to the next.
- whip pan: a fast pan with motion blur that carries into the next shot.
- crash zoom: a very fast zoom in or out that ends on the next shot.
- dissolve: one shot fades through the next.
- wipe: a shape or an edge moves across the frame and reveals the next shot.
- morph: one subject becomes the next with no cut.
- flash: a white or coloured frame between two shots, often on a hit.
- glitch: a digital break between two shots.
- mask reveal: the next shot shows through a shape, a letter or an object.

Camera moves:

- static: the camera does not move.
- push-in and pull-out: the camera moves toward or away from the subject.
- pan and tilt: the camera turns left, right, up or down.
- orbit: the camera circles the subject.
- tracking: the camera moves beside the subject.
- handheld drift: a small, loose movement.
- rack focus: the focus moves from one plane to another.
- speed ramp: the clip speeds up or slows down inside one shot.

Type behaviour:

- slam: a word lands at full size on a hit.
- type-on: letters appear one at a time.
- slide: a line moves in from an edge.
- scale-up: a line grows from small to full size.
- stack: lines build up one under the next.
- hold: a line stays still on screen.
